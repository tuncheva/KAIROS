What KAIROS should charge for

The agent layer is built and the meter already exists in the code. The open question is not how much — it is which line separates free from paid. This argues the line falls at proactivity, not at volume.

21 August 2026 Basis: shipped code, not projections Status: proposal

What actually costs money

The meter is model calls, and it is already built

Tasks, notes, events, comments and RSVPs are rows in Postgres. Their marginal cost rounds to zero. Every euro KAIROS spends per active user is an inference call, which means the pricing metric is not seats, projects or storage — it is turns.

That metric is already metered. rateLimit.ts runs a 24-hour sliding window per user, Redis-backed, and it splits the budget in two: what the user asks for, and what the system does on their behalf.

50 Interactive AI requests per user, per 24h AI_RATE_LIMIT
20 Proactive requests, on a separate budget AI_SYSTEM_RATE_LIMIT
0 Model cost to confirm and apply a plan no inference on write
3 Sub-agents one turn may run worst-case cost multiplier

The third figure is the one with pricing consequences. Generating a plan costs a model call; approving and applying it costs nothing. Acting on the AI is free — only asking is metered.

The line

Sell anticipation, not allowance

The usual move is to meter the free tier down and charge for more messages. That prices the thing users experience as a punishment, and it trains them to ration the feature you want them addicted to.

KAIROS has a better boundary sitting in its architecture. Five agents answer when spoken to. Two — the Daily Brief and the Risk Radar — run on a schedule and draw on their own budget, with no user in the loop. That is a categorical difference, not a quantitative one: one is a tool you pick up, the other is something that works while you sleep.

Free gets the full tool. Pro gets the thing that runs without you.

Three reasons this line is the right one:

  • It matches where cost is unbounded. Interactive spend is self-limiting — people only ask so much. Scheduled spend accrues every day for every opted-in user whether or not they open the app. Giving that away is the one commitment that scales badly.
  • It is legible. "It watches your projects and tells you what is slipping" is a sentence someone repeats to a colleague. "200 requests instead of 15" is not.
  • It does not cripple the free product. A free user gets all five conversational agents and all 21 tools. They form the habit on the full thing, which is what makes the upgrade feel like an addition rather than the removal of an artificial limit.

The tiers

Three tiers, one boundary each

Each step adds one thing you can name. Free ends where the product stops waiting to be asked; Pro ends where one person's workspace becomes several people's.

Annual billing at two months free (Pro €120/yr, Team €220/yr) is worth offering from day one — it converts the cash-flow question into a discount question.

Before you can charge

Two shipped limitations undercut the Pro pitch

Both are in the agent layer's own list of known gaps. They are tolerable in a free feature and embarrassing in a paid one, so they gate the launch rather than follow it.

Blocks Pro

The Daily Brief fires on UTC, not on your morning

ai_schedules.hourUtc stores an hour with no timezone. Bulgaria runs UTC+2 in winter and UTC+3 in summer, so a brief set for 07:00 arrives at 09:00 — and shifts by an hour twice a year without the user touching anything.

The Daily Brief is the headline Pro feature. "Your morning brief" that lands mid-morning and drifts seasonally is the first thing a paying customer will write in to complain about. This needs a real timezone column first.

Blocks Team

There is no cross-user view of anything

getAiMetrics is per-user, and the codebase has no admin role that spans tenants. Two Team rows above — org-wide Risk Radar and org-wide reporting — have no mechanism behind them today.

This is the larger of the two. It is why Team is drawn here as a later step rather than a launch tier: sell Free and Pro first, and let Team follow the admin role it depends on.

What has to be true

The assumptions this rests on

Each of these is falsifiable, and the first one is the one most likely to be wrong in a way that matters.

Inference stays close to free.
Why it might not KAIROS currently runs on free provider tiers. Those are rate-limited and can be withdrawn. Price for the cost of paid inference, not today's zero — at 200 requests/day a heavy Pro user is the entire margin. Model tiering (tier: "fast") is already in the client; route the cheap work through it before launch, not after.
15 free requests a day is enough to form a habit, and few enough to feel.
How to check Instrument how often free users hit the ceiling. If almost nobody does, the limit is doing no work and can rise; if most do on day one, it reads as a crippled trial rather than a real product.
€12 clears the bar in the launch market.
The risk It sits credibly against Notion AI and ClickUp Brain for a Western European buyer. For Bulgarian SMBs it may not — that is a market where per-seat SaaS faces real resistance. Consider a regional price rather than a global one.
Proactivity is what people pay for.
How to check It costs nothing to find out before building billing: offer the Daily Brief to a cohort, remove it after two weeks, and count who asks for it back. That is the cheapest possible test of this entire memo.

Implementation

Where this meets the code

The entitlement seam is already in place. entitlementsFor(ctx) in src/server/billing/entitlements.ts returns a flag set and currently grants everything to everyone, so the tiers above are describable in code today without blocking anyone.

Turning this memo on is three steps, in this order:

  • Add the flags. Extend the Entitlements interface with one field per boundary in the table — scheduled agents, agent pinning, undo, per-agent memory, request ceiling. Read them at each call site now, while they all return true and nothing can break.
  • Fix the two blockers. A timezone column on ai_schedules, and an admin role that can see across a tenant. Neither is billing work, and both are prerequisites rather than follow-ups.
  • Replace the constant. Swap the hardcoded return in entitlementsFor for a subscription lookup. Every call site already branches correctly by then, so this is the only file that changes.

Deliberately not on that list: the rate limiter needs no rework. It already reads its ceiling from configuration and already separates interactive from proactive spend, which is exactly the split the tiers are drawn along.
