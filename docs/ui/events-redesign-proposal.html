KAIROS · Design proposal · /publish

A feed with
people in it

The events surface has the mechanics of a social product — likes, RSVPs, comments, reminders, a live socket — and none of the social. Every visitor sees the same global list in the same order, newest first, because ordering is createdAt desc and nothing else. Meanwhile the follow graph shipped last week with the profile drawer: user_follows, profile.follow, profile.listFollows, an allowFollowers setting. Nothing on this page reads any of it. This proposal keeps every KAIROS token and rebuilds /publish around three moves — a feed that knows who you follow, a card that answers what, when, where before it asks for a reaction, and an event page that exists.

Where it stands

A working feed that is nobody’s in particular

The recent pass fixed the mechanics: the surface pages instead of growing forever, the engagement summary moved into a dialog, and two navigation panels came out of the right column. Those were the right repairs. They did not change what the page is — a chronological list of every event in the country, filtered by a dropdown of ten towns, where the only thing distinguishing your feed from a stranger's is which rows you happen to have RSVP'd to.

Already solid keep

  • Pagination that cannot skip a row. getPublicEvents orders by (createdAt desc, id desc) and carries both halves in the cursor, so two events created in the same millisecond still page correctly.
  • Optimistic writes with a real rollback. Likes and RSVPs paint immediately and restore the previous cache on failure, all against one FEED_QUERY_INPUT key so the reader and the patch cannot drift apart.
  • The RSVP uniqueness fix. rsvp_unique was a plain index despite its name; the check-then-insert in updateRsvp could race into two rows and count one person twice. It is a uniqueIndex now.
  • Counts that don't lie under pagination. getMySummary counts your RSVPs over the whole table, so the rail says "12 going" on page one instead of "2 going, ask again later".
  • Reminders left the browser. They were a setInterval per viewer against a mutation that returned success without sending anything. They come off the server scheduler now.
  • The selection rules are pure functions. feedData.ts holds filtering, banding and ordering with no React in sight, and is tested without mounting a tRPC provider.
  • The social primitives exist. user_follows is directed, unreciprocated and keyed to make a double-tap a no-op; resolveProfileAccess already decides who may see whom. The graph is built. It is just not plugged in.

What the surface gets wrong replace

  • The feed has no notion of relevance. One ordering, newest-first, for everyone. Following someone changes nothing about what you see. A feed that ignores its own follow graph is a bulletin board.
  • Search is written and unreachable. selectFeed takes a query and matches title, description, region and author — and PublishWorkspace calls it with query: "". There is no search input anywhere on the page. The feature is finished and hidden.
  • An event has a town, never a place. region is an enum of ten cities and is the only location column. No address, no venue, no coordinates, no room number. "Where is it" is not answerable, so nobody can travel to one of these.
  • No end time. One event_date timestamp. An event is "past" the instant it starts, so a three-day conference drops into Already happened on its opening morning.
  • There is no event page. /publish?event=12 scrolls a card into view inside a feed you must be signed in to reach. Nothing has its own URL, so nothing can be shared to a group chat, previewed with an OG image, or indexed.
  • Views filter in the browser. Going, Maybe and Hosting filter the rows the cursor happened to hand over, so the pager has to keep pulling pages to fill one screen. Those are three where clauses on a table that already has rsvp_user_idx.
  • Every card carries the host's machinery. EventCard is 664 lines and mounts the edit form, the RSVP dashboard and the reminder picker as portals per card — whether or not you own the event.
  • Covers fail silently. ALLOWED_IMAGE_HOSTS is utfs.io and lh3.googleusercontent.com; anything else renders as a text card with no explanation to whoever uploaded it.
  • "Tag Collaborators" does nothing. The create form renders a dashed + and the words Add guest hosts. There is no handler, no state, and no field for it in createEventSchema. Co-hosting is a drawn button.
  • Nothing groups events by kind. No topic, category or tag column, so "show me the tech meetups" is a question the schema cannot take.

Worth fixing before anything visual

Every comment on the page ships with the page. getPublicEvents fetches comments for the ten events with inArray and no limit, then hands the whole set to the client, which renders two and hides the rest behind View all. One popular event with four hundred comments makes every feed request that touches it enormous.

A new event never appears live. emitEventUpdated and emitEventDeleted fire from six call sites. createEvent emits nothing — there is no emitEventCreated — so the one moment a live feed exists for, it sits still.

The engagement dialog is honest but narrow. It says "across the events on screen" because that is all it can see: it sums the rows the cursor has loaded. As a host's analytics view it is measuring the reader's scroll position, not the event's reach.

Direction

Two lanes, one card, and a page to land on

The shape professional social products converge on is not complicated, and it is not what this page does. It is: a source you can switch (people you chose, or everything), a unit that is legible in one glance, and a destination with a URL for when the glance was enough to make you care.

So: the feed gets a Following / Discover segmented control where the Coming up / Already happened rule currently sits. Following is events hosted by people you follow, plus events your follows are going to — the graph already stores both halves. Discover is everything else, ranked rather than merely sorted: soonest first among upcoming, with your region and the people you follow breaking ties. Past events stop being a band in the middle of the feed and become a view in the rail, which is what they always were.

The card keeps its cover, date block and segmented RSVP, and gains the two things it is missing: a line saying why this is in front of you ("Maria you follow is going"), and the facts a person needs before they can decide — place, time, how many are going, whether it is nearly full. Comments leave the card entirely; two truncated comments under every event is what makes a long feed feel like a wall of noise, and they are what makes the payload heavy.

The event page at /events/[id] is where depth goes: full description, map, host card with a follow button, the attendee list, and the comment thread with replies. It renders for signed-out visitors, which is the difference between an event you can invite people to and an event only members can see.

Nothing below invents a colour, radius or shadow. Every surface is --bg-elevated or --bg-secondary, every rule is --hair, every accent is --accent-primary — which is user-selectable across six brand colours, so the mockups are drawn in the purple default and would follow a member who picks mint or sky.

The feed

Following, Discover, and a card you can act on

One frame, drawn at the width the surface actually gets after the recent layout pass: fixed rails and a feed column that takes what is left. Every state in it maps to something the system stores, or could store with the deltas listed further down — a follow, a reason line, an RSVP, a capacity, a page number.

The destination

An event that has its own address

Everything a card cannot hold goes here, and the page is public. A host who cannot paste a link into a group chat and have it open for people without accounts does not have an events product; they have an intranet noticeboard. This is also where the comment thread belongs — with replies, paginated, loaded when someone actually wants to read them.

Data model

Five columns, two tables, one index

Deliberately small. The follow graph is the expensive part and it is already built, which is what makes the rest of this proposal cheap.

ChangeShapeWhy
events.ends_attimestamptz nullNull keeps every existing row valid and reading as it does today. Banding switches from event_date < now to coalesce(ends_at, event_date) < now, so a multi-day event stops filing itself under past on its opening morning.

varchar(255)		The two lines a person needs to arrive. Both nullable; the card falls back to the region label it prints now.
events.capacityinteger nullNull means unlimited, which is every event today. Non-null turns on the places-left chip and the sold-out state.
events.topictopic enumOne nullable enum rather than a tags table — six or eight values covering what people actually post. A join table can come later if one value stops being enough.

event_cohosts(event_id, user_id) pkMakes the drawn Tag Collaborators button real. A co-host gets the host’s edit rights and appears on the page.
event_saves(event_id, user_id) pkBookmarking without an RSVP. People are currently using Maybe for this, which corrupts the host’s counts.
event_date_idxextendRanked discovery orders by date, not creation. The index exists on event_date already; discovery wants (event_date asc, id asc) for a stable cursor in the other direction.

Not proposed, on purpose

No ticketing, no payments, no waitlist. Capacity is a number and a chip. The moment money is involved this stops being a feed proposal and becomes a billing one, and billing.ts is somebody else’s surface.

No recurring events. Recurrence rules are a well-known trap — every one of them ends in a per-occurrence exceptions table. Worth its own proposal, not a column here.

Four procedures change shape, three are new, one socket emit is missing. Nothing is removed — getPublicEvents stays as the discovery path so the migration is additive.

Mobile

The rails become a sheet, the card stays whole

The feed is the one surface in this app that is genuinely mobile-first in use — people read it standing up, at the event, on the way to it. Both rails collapse: views and filters into a sheet behind the filter button, the aside into nothing (invitations belong in notifications on a phone). The card keeps every part of its desktop anatomy at the same proportions, because it is already a vertical unit.

Delivery

Five phases, shippable in order

Ordered so that the two cheapest phases are also the two that change the page most, and so nothing needs a migration before it earns one.

01

Unhide what is already built no migration

Wire the search input to the query that selectFeed already accepts. Add emitEventCreated and listen for it. Cap the comments in getPublicEvents and drop them from the card. Put a Follow button on the card header against the existing profile.follow. Four small changes, and one of them fixes the feed payload.

02

The Following lane no migration

event.getFeed with the two sources, the follow joins, and the reason line. Server-side Going / Maybe / Hosting filters at the same time, since they are where clauses on the query being written anyway. The rail keeps its counts; the pager stops having to pull cursors to fill a page.

03

The event page migration

/events/[id] as a public route with getById, getComments and OG tags. This is where ends_at, venue, address and parent_id land — the page is the first thing that can show them. The feed card starts linking to it instead of scrolling to itself.

04

Topics, capacity, co-hosts migration

The three columns that change what the composer asks for. Topic chips in the rail, the places-left chip on the card, and the Tag Collaborators button finally doing something. Co-hosts inherit edit rights, which means one permission check in updateEvent and deleteEvent.

05

Saves, suggestions, host analytics migration

event_saves so people stop using Maybe as a bookmark. profile.getSuggestions for the who-to-follow panel. And the engagement dialog re-pointed at a per-event query so a host sees their event’s reach rather than the reader’s scroll position.

Open questions

Decisions this proposal does not make

  • Which lane opens by default? The mockup opens on Following, which is right for someone with fifty follows and empty for someone with none. The likely answer is Discover until you follow five people, then Following — but that is a rule someone has to own, and a first-run state to design.
  • How honest should the reason line be? “3 people you follow are going” discloses RSVPs to a wider audience than the card does today. resolveProfileAccess can gate the names, but the count still leaks something. Names for full-access profiles and a bare count otherwise is the assumption here.
  • Is the region enum still the right shape? Ten hardcoded towns cannot express a village, and adding one is a migration. If events acquire a real address, the enum becomes a coarse filter over something more precise — worth deciding before venue ships, not after.
  • Do past events belong in the feed at all? This proposal moves them to a rail view. For a host they are archive; for a guest they are noise. But an event with photos and forty comments afterwards is exactly the kind of thing a social feed should keep showing.
  • What does a public event page mean for private workspaces? Every event is public today because there is no visibility column, so a public route changes nothing technically — but it makes that fact visible, and someone may have been assuming otherwise. Worth confirming before the route ships rather than after.
  • Topic as an enum or a tags table? One nullable enum is proposed because it is cheap and filterable. If events routinely span two topics, that decision has to be revisited with a join table and a migration behind it.
  • Does the composer survive? It is a title field and three chips that open the real dialog. With topic, venue and capacity to ask for, the dialog grows — and the inline composer may be better as a single button.
